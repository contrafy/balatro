Post-mortem:
- Binding constraint: scaling, compounded by bad luck on the final draw. The engine (Two Pair L3 + Full House L1, 5 flat/PLUS jokers) was strong enough through ante 4 (12188/10000 boss) but the ante-5 Small Blind target (11000) needed >2x margin per hand to survive a weak final draw, and our margins were much thinner (est just barely clearing or falling short repeatedly from ante 3 onward: 1628, 1584, 1220 vs need, etc.).
- CRITICAL FINDING: Baseball Card ("x1.5 per uncommon joker owned") was bought at ante 4 as "the xMult" per shopadvise's XMULT label, but every other joker in the roster (Clever, Crafty, Jolly, Jolly, Wily, Mad) is rarity 1 (Common) -- confirmed via `raw` (each shows `"rarity": 1`). Baseball Card's own condition counts UNCOMMON jokers, and we owned ZERO. This means Baseball Card contributed x1.5^0 = x1 (literally nothing) for the entire back half of the run. We believed we had "secured xMult by ante 4" per the task's hard rule, but in practice we had a joker slot occupied by a decoration that never fired. This is very likely the actual reason ante 5 was too tight: with a real xMult active the Small Blind would have cleared with room to spare.
- Decision I would change: before buying Baseball Card, check how many Uncommon-rarity jokers are already owned (via `raw`'s `rarity` field, since the state line doesn't show rarity) -- with 0 uncommon jokers owned, Baseball Card is a dead pick and a genuinely reliable xMult (or an Uncommon joker to activate it) should have been hunted instead, even at the cost of another reroll cycle.
