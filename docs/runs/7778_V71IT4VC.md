Post-mortem: binding constraint = zero scoring jokers against a Green Stake Big Blind (450) with pure card
variance. Every discard/play matched `advise`'s est exactly (no >20% est/actual deviations to report), so
this was not a misplay or a bridge bug - it was the well-documented stake-3 ante-1-2 zero-joker failure mode
(playbook 7a: "STAKE 3 ANTE 1-2 ... died at ante 1-2 with ZERO scoring jokers"). The straight (hand 1) and
flush chase (hand 3) were reasonable EV plays per `advise`'s own math, but this run simply never drew a
joker-free path to 450 in 5 hands. The one decision I would reconsider: on hand 2, `advise` discarded the
made King pair entirely to chase a straight with EV 81 (still less than the Pair's own est 60 once card
chips are added, and well under the 170 needed either way) while a full discard was still in the bank
afterward. In hindsight the EV gap between the two options was tiny and the King pair discard didn't clearly
help; it's not obviously wrong, but it never had a chance to matter since neither path could reach 450 in
one hand and no shop was reached to gain a joker before the pressure hit.

Bridge issues: none. Every command returned as documented; `est` matched `actual` scored exactly on both
hands that were played (280/280 and, cumulatively, 64/64 and 60/60), so no >20% deviations occurred.

Playbook suggestions:
1. The Skip-Small-Blind-at-ante-1 rule (regardless of tag except Boss) trades a $0 blind for a shop visit,
   but with zero jokers the Big Blind then has to be cleared in exactly 3 "real" hands (5 dealt-hand cycles
   with discards) with no joker help. Consider noting explicitly that skipping ante-1 Small Blind is a
   trade of one loss-mode (300 in 3 hands) for another, slightly less likely, loss-mode (450 in the same 3
   hands but now with cushion from discards) - the data already shows both fail some fraction of the time,
   so quantifying "skip win rate" vs "play win rate" at stake 3 specifically (not just stake 2) would help
   future agents judge whether skipping is still net-positive at Green Stake's faster scaling.
2. No change needed to the discard/chase logic itself - it performed exactly as documented and all
   estimates were exact - but it may be worth flagging in section 7a that a run which skips the ante-1 Small
   Blind and then loses the Big Blind has spent its entire run (5 hand-cycles) without ever reaching a shop,
   which is a strictly worse outcome (in terms of information/preparation for a next run) than playing and
   losing the Small Blind - future agents comparing skip vs. play should weigh that a Big Blind loss ends
   the run just as surely as a Small Blind loss would have, but with less logged shop data.
