Post-mortem: Binding constraint was SCALING vs the ante-6 curve, compounded by a misplay/sequencing issue: all 3 discards were spent early hunting marginal two-pair/flush draws instead of preserving them to guarantee a Full House-caliber hand on a later draw. Full House L7 (190x16) plus Triboulet is a strong engine when the deal cooperates (three-of-a-kind + pair), but the deck only delivered pairs this blind, and with 0 discards left for the final 3 hands there was no way to fish for the third card of a trips. The single xMult source (Triboulet, K/Q-conditional) was also never supplemented by a second, more general xMult joker despite 6 shop visits - the playbook's own data (7a) says this is expected (xMult rarely appears) but this run had no fallback plan for the case where the main-hand full house simply doesn't materialize card-wise.
One decision I would change: on the Big Blind of Ante 6, after the first hand (Pair T, est 4860, scored) left 3 hands and 0 discards to cover a 25140 gap, I should have recognized the math was already failing (need ~8000+/hand average with pair-only hands topping out near 5000) and there was nothing left to "play around" - at that point the outcome was effectively fixed by the earlier discard sequencing, not the final hands. The real fix was upstream: on the 3rd discard of that hand (before the "0 discards left" state), keep the QQ pair intact instead of breaking it to chase a diamond flush that only had 4 cards toward it - the discard that broke the Q pair (disc 1 3 6) sacrificed a guaranteed Two Pair/Full-House lever for a low-probability flush that never landed.

Playbook suggestions:
1. When joker slots (6/6) AND consumable slots (2/2, all unused) are both full, shopadvise should flag packs as SKIP/AVOID rather than BUY, or suggest selling a dead consumable first - this run wasted a $4 Arcana Pack buy with no valid destination for any of its 3 cards.
2. For hands with 0 discards remaining and multiple hands left, `advise` could project the required per-hand average against the actual candidate-hand ceiling (not just the current best hand) and flag "unwinnable at this scoring ceiling" earlier, so the operator knows to treat every subsequent decision as damage control rather than optimization.
3. Full-House/pair-based single-xMult (Triboulet-style, rank-conditional) engines are fragile against boss/big blinds that don't deal trips - consider explicitly recommending a secondary conditional/backup hand type (e.g., Two Pair leveled high, or a flush suit kept live) be held in reserve via discard discipline rather than spent chasing draws mid-round, especially once total joker/consumable slots are maxed and no further engine changes are possible.
