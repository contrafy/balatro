Post-mortem: binding constraint was SCALING, not the flush chase itself. Two Pair (our de-facto main hand from ante 1)
  was never leveled all run -- no Uranus/Celestial-pack level was ever bought, only flat/xMult jokers (Green Joker,
  Hologram) and a one-off Tower tarot. At L1 (20x2), Two Pair could not keep pace with the ante-2 Big Blind (1200)
  even with 3 jokers; a leveled main hand would have scored meaningfully more on every play including the forced weak
  ones. The decision I would change: at the ante-2 shop with $9, buy Jupiter ($3, flush/secondary-hand planet was
  offered, no Two Pair planet was) or Jumbo Celestial Pack ($6) for a hand level BEFORE Hologram ($7), or split money
  across both instead of spending $7 of $9 on a single xMult joker while the main hand sat unleveled at L1. Also: the
  flush chase used 3 of the round's discards without ever completing, and once discards ran out an unlucky no-pair
  deal cost an entire hand of scoring -- with hindsight, converting one flush-chase discard into an earlier Two Pair
  commit would have preserved a discard for the final hand.

Playbook suggestions:
1. Fix/flag the `est` model for Stone cards used as fillers: the Tower tarot converts a filler card to a Stone card
   specifically to add chips without joining the scoring set, and this run showed its chips DO count in the actual
   score (+170 delta) while `est` credited zero. If confirmed as intended Balatro mechanics, `hands`/`advise` should
   add a Stone-filler's flat chips to every candidate hand's est.
2. Add an explicit rule to section 5 (ante 1-2): if the main hand has not been leveled by the end of ante 2's first
   shop, treat a Celestial pack / that hand's planet as equal priority to an xMult joker purchase, not subordinate to
   it -- this run bought a $7 xMult joker over a $3-6 level-up and lost the very next blind to insufficient scaling.
3. Section 4 discard guidance: when a flush/straight chase has already burned 2+ discards without completing and
   discards are down to the last one, prefer banking the made pair/two-pair over one more speculative discard,
   especially early in a round with hands still in reserve -- running out of discards mid-round with weak dealt cards
   (no pairs at all) cost this run an entire hand's score with zero recourse.
