## Post-mortem
- Binding constraint: bad luck compounded with only one scoring joker (Wily Joker, which never got to
  fire because we never drew a Three of a Kind again after the ante-1 Small Blind full house) and a flush
  chase that never connected. Every `est` matched the actual score exactly (0% error) in every hand this
  run - the estimator was perfectly accurate; the loss was pure draw variance on the Big Blind, not a
  miscalculation.
- Decision I would change: at the second discard round of the Big Blind (hand showing QD 9S 9H 7C 5S 5C
  7S... i.e. 4 spades held with only 1 discard left), the flush chase left us needing a 5th spade among 4
  redrawn cards while also giving up the sure Two Pair line earlier. In hindsight, once the made Two Pair
  (104 est on the first hand) was already below the per-hand budget needed (450/3 ~ 150/hand), the flush
  chase was the mathematically correct EV move per `advise`, and I would not override it - but I would
  flag that a single scoring joker (Wily, a 3-of-a-kind-only conditional) is thin insurance for the Big
  Blind when a Small Blind full house was pure luck; a second, more general +mult joker (e.g. a Jolly/Joker
  card that fires on any pair) would have raised every one of these Two Pair hands (104/68/120) enough to
  clear 450 without needing the flush gamble at all.
- Bridge issues: none. Every command returned as documented; no `ACTION FAILED`, no retries needed,
  no Connection refused, no `est` mismatch anywhere in this run.
- Playbook suggestions:
  1. Section 5 (ante 1-2 shop): the Buffoon Pack choice of "conditional-type joker (Wily/Jolly/Sly/etc,
     each fires on only one specific poker hand type)" vs a same-tier alternative should note that
     3-of-a-kind-only jokers (Wily, Mad) are weaker insurance than pair-triggered jokers (Jolly, Sly) for
     ante 1-2 hands, which are overwhelmingly pairs/two-pair rather than trips - this run's Wily Joker
     never fired again after the opening full house.
  2. Section 4 flush-chase guidance: when a flush chase (`advise`'s EV-based override) fails to complete
     and forces a fallback to Two Pair on the last discard, and 0 discards + 1 hand remain with the made
     hand still short of `need`, the playbook should flag this as effectively a lost blind before the
     final hand is played, so the log documents "unwinnable state reached" as a distinct failure mode
     from misplay - useful for tallying how often flush chases cost the run versus how often they win it.
