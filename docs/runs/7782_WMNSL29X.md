## Post-mortem

Best result this run: stake 2, ante 5, lost to the boss blind The Head (22000 needed, scored 13340 total across 3 hands). This is one ante deeper than the run this instance inherited (previous run lost ante 4 Big Blind). Final build: Two Pair L2 (main hand, was L3 before The Arm de-leveled it twice at ante 4 and one Uranus at ante 5 only restored it to L2), Full House L4 (secondary hand, fed by 3 Earth planets since Uranus rarely appeared in Celestial packs this run), 6 jokers (Misprint, Joker +4, Scholar, Sock and Buskin, Mad Joker +10 if Two Pair, Photograph x2 first face - ZERO reliable xMult sources the whole run; Photograph is the closest thing and it is conditional), plus 2 Empress-enhanced Queens (+4 mult) and 2 Hierophant-enhanced 5s (+30 chips) scattered in the deck, one Lucky-carded Jack pair (from a desperation ante-4 Magician use) that kept reappearing and paying off.

Money curve: $4 (start) -> $8 (end ante1) -> $10 (end ante2) -> $21 (end ante3) -> $17 (end ante4) -> $7 (end ante5, spent down each shop per shopadvise's under-scaled guidance) -> $3 (final).

The one decision I would change: at ante 3-4, when Uranus (Two Pair's planet) kept failing to appear in Celestial packs, I fed the fallback Earth planet into Full House three separate times (L2->L3->L4) instead of ever finding a genuine xMult joker or pack. That produced two decent-but-unleveraged pair-family hands and no multiplier at all — by ante 5 the run had 0 real xMult sources, which is exactly the failure mode section 7a warns about ("zero xMult at ante 5 loses"). With the benefit of hindsight I would have reroll-hunted harder for Photograph+Pareidolia support, an xMult joker, or Justice (for Glass cards) rather than accepting 3 Celestial-pack Earth levels as "scaling" — none of that showed up in the shop this run, but the run also never rerolled specifically hunting for it once past ante 2, relying on shopadvise's ADVICE line which itself never flagged an affordable reroll-for-xMult opportunity. The Arm boss at ante 4 costing us 2 levels of Two Pair (from playing it twice before switching to fresh Straight/Straight for the rest of the round) was also a real cost — a cleaner Arm plan would have been to open with a fresh unleveled type FIRST (before ever risking the leveled main hand) whenever the starting hand allows it, saving the level hit entirely.

Bridge issues: none. Every action returned a clean state or an expected `(est N vs actual M: logged for calibration)` line; no `ACTION FAILED` or connection errors this run. The `order` command worked fine even outside SHOP phase (used once in BLIND_SELECT after forgetting to reorder before `endshop`), which was a useful fallback.

Playbook suggestions:
1. Misprint (+0..23 random mult) produced calibration misses of -29%, +77%, +37%, +34%, +38% across this single run — every one of the 20%+ misses this run had Misprint in the joker list. The playbook's "conservative 6, mean 11" estimate is fine as a floor but the variance is large enough (roughly 400-2500+ chip swings depending on level) that `advise`'s "still N short, 0/1 hands left" framing on a forced final hand should explicitly flag "includes a live Misprint roll — actual could be up to 2x/0.5x est" so the agent knows not to concede prematurely nor over-trust a razor-thin PASS.
2. When a Celestial/Jumbo Celestial pack repeatedly (3+ times in one run) fails to offer the main hand's planet and only offers a secondary hand's planet, `shopadvise`/pack advice could suggest actively rerolling shop stock (not just the pack contents) rather than banking further levels into the secondary hand — after the first "not your main hand" warning, a second and third one probably means it's time to look for the main-hand planet directly in a future shop's card row instead of gambling on more packs.
3. Under The Arm, `advise` already suggests unleveled types, but it could go further: when the FIRST hand of the round already contains a usable unleveled type (e.g., a Straight/Flush draw alongside a leveled Two Pair), prefer that ordering explicitly (unleveled type first, leveled type only if forced) rather than only switching after the leveled hand has already been played and de-leveled once.
