Post-mortem: binding constraint was scaling, not misplay-in-the-obvious-sense: only 4 jokers (2 flat: +4 mult / +3mult-per-Diamond, 2 xMult: Card Sharp x3-if-repeat, Blackboard x3-if-all-held-cards-black) going into a 3000-need Big Blind at ante 3, and neither xMult joker fired even once during that blind. Card Sharp never fired because the three hands played (Two Pair, Pair, Straight) were three different hand types with zero repeats -- `advise`'s discard chain (from AK/KT two-pair candidates down to AT) optimized purely for highest immediate est each step and never considered sequencing two hands of the SAME type to unlock Card Sharp's x3, even though playbook section 4.6 explicitly calls this out ("play a cheap version of the type first, then the strong versions"). Blackboard also never fired in this blind because the discards needed to build the two-pair kept red cards (hearts/diamonds) in the final held hand, whereas in the Small Blind of the same ante the flush chase happened to leave only spades/clubs behind and got a full x3 (est 800 -> 2640) purely as a byproduct. The one decision I would change: on the first hand of a big-need blind with a Card Sharp-type joker and only 3 hands total, deliberately play the SAME hand type twice in a row (e.g. Pair, then Two Pair, or Two Pair, then Two Pair again if the deal allows) even at some est cost on the first play, specifically to bank the x3 on the second/third play, rather than always taking the single highest-est candidate each turn.

Bridge issues: none this run -- every command executed as expected, no `ACTION FAILED` retries needed, no misreads of hand type.

Playbook suggestions:
1. Add explicit guidance to section 4 (or 4.6): when a Card Sharp-style "x3 if type already played this round" joker is owned and only 2-3 hands remain in a blind, actively sequence PLAYS of the same hand type (not just discards) to guarantee at least one x3 proc, even if the first play's est is lower than an alternative different-type hand -- `advise` currently always recommends the single highest-est hand/discard per turn and does not look ahead to type-repetition payoff.
2. Add a Blackboard-specific note near section 4/6: Blackboard's (and similar "if all held cards are X" jokers') condition depends on what is LEFT in hand after the play, not just what's played -- when discarding to build a made hand, prefer discarding cards of the "wrong" suit even if they are not needed for the hand itself, since keeping a stray red card while chasing a black-suit archetype silently kills the x3 bonus. `advise`'s discard chains in this run (down to AT two pair) left red cards in hand and lost the proc that had fired identically in the same ante's Small Blind.
3. Consider flagging in `shopadvise`/`advise` output when 0 discards remain and the projected sum of remaining hands (shown as "still N short") cannot mathematically clear the blind -- this run's final two hands both printed "still short" messages but nothing suggested an alternative (there wasn't one at that point, but earlier surfacing at the discard-decision stage that the Two Pair path could not reach 3000 in 3 hands might have prompted holding out for Card Sharp setup instead).
