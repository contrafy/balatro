Post-mortem: binding constraint was card-draw variance combined with money/xMult scarcity, not a misplay. The run
never found a real xMult joker (Photograph's face-card x2 was the only one, conditional); the engine relied entirely
on hand levels (Two Pair L4, Full House L4) plus Glass/Bonus card enhancements, which scales additively/geometrically
per hand but has no floor when the cards drawn happen to be a weak single pair with no second pair to pivot to. The
losing Big Blind drew three consecutive weak hands (trips-of-4 twice, then a 9-high straight) with no pair-family
follow-up material and zero discards left to fix it. The one decision I would change: at the ante 6 shop before this
blind, I twice took The Hermit tarot from Arcana Packs for a trivial $2->$4 money bump instead of skipping to save
the $3 pack cost, or aggressively rerolling for an xMult joker/second Justice/Chariot enhancement (we had jokers
full at 6/6 the whole back half of the run and never found reason per shopadvise to swap out Shoot the Moon, our
weakest slot, for a real xMult candidate like Supernova/The Tribe/Ride the Bus when they appeared in ante 4 and 6
shops - all were skipped because "jokers full, no clear sell target" per shopadvise, but with hindsight Shoot the
Moon (a Queen-count conditional PLUS, not xMult) was the correct sell candidate and was never proposed as one).

Playbook suggestions:
1. When a Celestial pack's fallback planet keeps landing on a non-main, non-pair-family hand (Mars hit Four of a
   Kind twice this run, unrequested), note explicitly that Four of a Kind is NOT part of the "pair family" fallback
   set (Pair/Two Pair/Full House) so agents don't rationalize taking it as pair-family-adjacent.
2. Add a rule: when `shopadvise` lists a real or conditional xMult joker (Supernova, The Tribe, Ride the Bus, Smiley
   Face, etc.) while joker slots are full (6/6) and the roster's weakest joker is a narrow-conditional PLUS (e.g. a
   "+mult per <rare card held>" joker like Shoot the Moon that rarely has 2+ qualifying cards in hand), explicitly
   suggest selling that narrow-conditional joker to make room, rather than defaulting to LEAVE. This run kept a
   weak conditional PLUS joker in the last slot through antes 4-6 instead of ever swapping toward an xMult.
3. Note the est-undershoot pattern for stacked Glass/Photograph/suit-mult hands (see above) so agents don't
   under-plan boss-hand safety margins when they estimate a hand will "barely" clear - actual often runs 20-60%
   higher, but the reverse (actual running short) is also possible when card draw luck is unfavorable, so this
   should not be relied upon as a safety margin, only noted as a modeling gap to eventually fix in `est`.
